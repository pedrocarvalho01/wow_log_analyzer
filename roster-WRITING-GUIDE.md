# Writing Guide: Rationale, Notes and Descriptions

This guide covers the text the agent writes: the **Rationale** column, **Notes**, **Proposed composition**, the header subtitle and the chat summary. The goal is text that is **brief but informative**. Every line should tell the reader *why*, backed by a number or a rank, in words a raid leader can act on.

The rationales and notes are generated by `wcl/writing.py` from the signals in `wcl/signals.py` and the decisions in `wcl/rank.py` (see `roster-DECISION-GUIDE.md`). Any prose the agent adds itself, such as the chat summary, follows the same rules. Layout and styling are covered in `roster-onepager-STYLE-SPEC.md`; this file only covers the text.

---

## 1. The core problem to avoid

Rationales fail in two ways:

| | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Too thin** | "Low DPS" | Doesn't say *how* low, compared to whom, or what else counts |
| **Restating the columns** | "148.8k DPS, 87.9% survival" | The reader can already see these numbers |
| **Target** | "Lowest DPS and item level (318); second-lowest survival in the raid; lowest active time" | Ranks the player, adds information not in the table (item level) and explains the decision |

**Rule:** a rationale **interprets** the numbers (rank, comparison, consequence). It never just repeats them.

---

## 2. Step 1: compute signals before writing

Do not write from raw metrics. For every player, first build a `signals` object (`wcl/signals.py`). The writing step only uses these signals.

Comparisons use what the reader can see: **raw DPS/HPS as shown in the table, within the role.** Protected players are left out of every comparison, so no statement about someone else depends on a protected player's numbers. Excluded players (support specs, too few pulls) are left out of the output ranking.

| Signal | How it is computed | Phrase it produces |
|---|---|---|
| `output_rank` | Rank of DPS/HPS within the role, from the top and from the bottom | "Highest", "Second-highest", "Third-highest", "Lowest", "Second-lowest", "Third-lowest" |
| `output_vs_median` | player / role median − 1 | "High" (> +8%), "Above-average" (+3 to 8%), "Solid" / "Average" (±3%), "Below-average" (−3 to −8%), "Well below-average" (< −8%) |
| `lead_margin` | For #1 in the role: gap to #2 | "by a clear margin" if > 5% |
| `near_tie_peer` | Recorded by the decision step for close calls | "on par with {peer}" |
| `survival_rank` | Rank of survival % across the whole raid, among players who died | "lowest survival in the raid", "second-lowest survival in the raid", "among the higher death rates" (bottom 25%) |
| `no_deaths` | No deaths in any pull | "no deaths" |
| `lowest_active` | Lowest active % in the role, and below 90% | "lowest active time" |
| `ilvl_low` | Item level ≥ 5 below the raid median | "item level ({ilvl})", merged into "Lowest DPS and item level" when both are the lowest |
| `dtaken_high` | Damage-taken rate > role mean + 1 SD | "high damage taken ({x}m)"; "highest damage taken among DPS ({x}m, {pct} mitigated)" for the highest, when mitigation is in the role's lowest quarter |
| `dtaken_low` | Damage taken in the role's lowest quarter | "low damage taken" |
| `overheal` | Healer overhealing > 45%, or < 30% | "high overhealing ({x}%)" / "low overhealing" |
| `slot_position` | Healers and tanks, when the role has more players than slots: position from the top | "Fifth healer", "Fourth healer", "third tank" |
| `gap_to_next` | For a healer or tank cut: gap to the lowest player kept on merit | "6% below Ashiea" |
| `class_redundancy` | 3+ players of the class and this player is the weakest of them | "fourth Mage on the roster" |
| `sample` | Attended < 70% of pulls | "attended {n} of {m} pulls" |
| `tank_load` | The tank who took the most damage in the raid | "absorbs most damage in raid ({x}m)" |

Early deaths are not used in rationales; they feed the pattern note (§5).

## 3. Step 2: choose what to say (selection logic)

Pick **2–3 signals**, ordered by how much they matter for this player's status. A topic already covered (for example survival, in a near-tie clause) is not repeated.

- **Remove:**
  1. The *decisive reason* for the cut: the near-tie comparison, the slot position for healers and tanks ("Fifth healer"), or the output rank ("Lowest DPS").
  2. Evidence that supports the decision: survival, item level, active time, class redundancy, damage taken, sample, in that order.
- **Reserve (DPS):** the reason they are close to the cut line, plus the reason they were kept. Example: "Well below-average DPS offset by good survival". If nothing offsets it, name the weakness and end with "next in line for a cut".
- **Reserve (healer or tank):** the output rank if in the top three, otherwise the slot position, plus one caveat. Example: "Fourth healer; high overhealing (52.0%)".
- **Keep, with a clear weakness:** strength first, then up to two caveats. Example: "Above-average DPS; high death rate and damage taken (245m)". The reader needs to see the risk.
- **Keep, no weakness:** name the one or two standout strengths. Example: "Third-highest DPS with no deaths".
- **Keep, unremarkable:** say it plainly: "Consistent, reliable performance". Do not invent a strength.
- **Essential (tank):** the role's job and one proof. Example: "Main tank; absorbs most damage in raid (539m), 46.4% mitigation"; other tanks: "Stable tanking with 50.9% mitigation".
- **Excluded:** "Augmentation Evoker; own DPS understates contribution, excluded from the cut", or "Attended 3 of 15 pulls; too few to rank, excluded from the cut".
- **Composition swap:** "Cut so the roster keeps battle res coverage; …" / "Kept for battle res coverage; …".
- **Protected:** always exactly `Not evaluated`. Never mention their numbers anywhere.

**Near-tie rule:** if the player was cut because of a close call, the rationale **must name the peer**:

- decided on survival: "DPS on par with Shalammage, but a notably higher death rate"
- decided on class: "DPS on par with {peer}, but the fourth Mage on the roster"
- otherwise: "DPS within 3% of {peer}; close call for the raid leader"

This is what makes the decision easy to defend.

## 4. Step 3: write the rationale (format rules)

- **Length:** 3–16 words, at most 3 clauses separated by **semicolons**.
- **Numbers:** at least one concrete number or rank word ("lowest", "second-highest", "above-average"). Only "Consistent, reliable performance" and support-spec lines are exempt.
- Put the **decisive factor first**.
- **Sentence case, no trailing period, no subject.** Write "Lowest DPS…", not "He has the lowest DPS…". No pronouns at all.
- **Superlatives are always relative to the role.** For survival, say "in the raid", because it compares the whole raid.
- **Numbers in parentheses** go directly after the fact they support: "item level (318)", "high damage taken (248m)".
- **Contrast connectors:** use "but" or "offset by" when a strength and a weakness appear together.
- **Allowed intensifiers:** "notably", "well", "by a clear margin", and only when the threshold in §2 is met.
- **Banned:** vague judgments ("bad", "poor player", "needs to improve"), speculation about skill, effort or attitude, anything about attendance or behavior that isn't in the logs, "(s)" plurals, emoji, exclamation marks, and first person.

## 5. Notes section

Write **2–4 bullets**, each **15–30 words**, full sentences ending in a period.

Every bullet follows the same structure: **observation → evidence (names or numbers) → implication or next step.**

Choose bullets from these categories, in priority order, and use each category at most once:

1. **Data caveat that could change a decision.** Examples: support spec (Augmentation), low attendance sample.
   > "Erythria (Augmentation) was excluded because the spec's value shows in other players' damage and Denixirian attended only 10 of 15 pulls; both verdicts should be confirmed by the raid leader."
2. **An actionable issue among players who stay.** Use a pattern across several players rather than a single one: 3 or more DPS with high damage taken, or 3 or more players with 2+ early deaths.
   > "Panyc, Darksaiko, Nastra and Hiroseer take well above the DPS average (~225m); an ability breakdown may reveal avoidable damage."
3. **What the analysis did not measure, and which decision it could affect.** Always name the specific decision: every close call, or, if there are none, the tightest cut and the player just above it.
   > "Utility (interrupts, dispels, assignments) was not assessed and may inform the Hasizawa / Ashiea decision."
4. **Composition risk after the cuts.** Only include this if it is real: a coverage swap, a lost buff or Bloodlust source, a short role.

If fewer than two categories apply, add: "Attendance history, communication and attitude are not in the logs and should be weighed before the {names} cuts are confirmed."

Notes rules:

- **Never repeat a rationale.** Notes are about patterns and caveats that go beyond a single row.
- **Always name who** is involved and **give a number** where one exists (an average, a threshold, a count).
- End each bullet with **what to do or check**: "should be confirmed", "may reveal…", "may inform the X / Y decision".
- **Never** comment on protected players, and never speculate about behavior or attitude.

---

## 6. Proposed composition block

- Use the labels `Tanks (n):`, `Healers (n):`, `DPS (n):` and `Removed:` exactly, with the labels in bold.
- Within each role, list names **from best to worst** by rank. Excluded players (support specs, too few pulls) are not ranked on merit, so they come last in their role. The removed players are listed in their rank order.
- Separate names with commas and a space. There is no "and" before the last name and no period at the end.
- Protected players appear in their role list like anyone else, with no marker.

---

## 7. Header subtitle

Use the fixed template, filling in the real values:

> Based on combined data from all pulls (kills and wipes), ignoring events after {cutoff} player deaths. Players are compared within their role: DPS by damage per second, healers by healing per second, tanks as essential slots. Survival and damage taken serve as secondary criteria. Ranked from **#1 (lowest performance)** to **#{N} (highest)**.

For a single fight, the first sentence starts "Based on data from a single pull (fight {id})".

## 8. Spelling and language

- **Player names:** copy them exactly as returned by the API, byte for byte, including uncommon letters (Lifèstream, Enzð, Välerjar). Never ASCII-fold, "correct" or re-capitalize a name: "Enzð" uses the letter eth (ð), and that is the name on the character profile. The pipeline is UTF-8 end to end, and the CSV is written with a BOM so Excel shows these names correctly.
- **English variant:** use **American English** consistently (e.g. "analyze", "color", "prioritize", "normalized").
- **Terminology:** DPS, HPS, item level (spelled out in prose; "ilvl" is fine only in the CSV), overhealing, mitigation, pull, wipe, raid leader.
- **Class names:** Death Knight, Demon Hunter, Druid, Evoker, Hunter, Mage, Monk, Paladin, Priest, Rogue, Shaman, Warlock, Warrior. Always capitalized.
- **Ordinals in words:** "second-highest", "third-highest", "fourth Mage", "fifth healer". Hyphenate compound ordinals.
- **Numbers:** `148.8k`, `87.9%`, `46.4%`, `254m`, `100%`, not `100.0%`.
- **Grammar:** agreement with player names, "among DPS" (not "among DPSs"), lists without a serial comma ("Nastra and Hiroseer"), and no double spaces.
- **Spelling:** the generated prose comes only from fixed phrases in `wcl/writing.py`, so it is checked by the tests rather than by a spell checker at run time.

## 9. Self-review pass (mandatory)

Every rationale and note is checked after writing (`validate_rationale` / `validate_note` in `wcl/writing.py`). Failures print as `Writing check:` lines, and the agent must fix or flag them. Checks marked **auto** run on every report; the others are covered by the tests.

- [ ] **auto:** each rationale contains at least one rank word or number (except "Consistent, reliable performance" and support-spec lines).
- [ ] **auto:** each rationale is 3–16 words, sentence case, no trailing period, no pronoun, no banned wording.
- [ ] **auto:** each note is 15–30 words, ends with a period, and never names a protected player.
- [ ] **auto:** protected players read exactly "Not evaluated".
- [ ] Each Remove rationale explains *why this player* and not the next one (decisive factor first; gap or peer named for close calls and healer cuts).
- [ ] Every superlative is true: "lowest" really is the lowest among the players the reader can compare.
- [ ] Notes name players, give numbers and end with an action.
- [ ] Player names are untouched.
